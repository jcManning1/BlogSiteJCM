Hamlet on the Holodeck Ch.3

Hamlet on the Holodeck Ch.3 lands with uncommon clarity. Murray argues we are stuck in an “additive” phase of digital media; treating the internet like a personalized scrapbook that layers video, text, and interaction on top of old forms. Her comparison to early cinema is especially useful. Just as filmmakers stopped filming stage plays and invented techniques that only film could do, we need to stop designing digital works as extensions of books or films. The medium’s true expressive essence only appears when we have treated its native abilities/capacities as the primary substances rather than embellishments.

What intrigues me is Murray’s explanation/theory of the four key properties of digital environments; procedural, participatory, spatial, and encyclopedic. ELIZA is a great early example. I repeatedly catch myself designing for the screen like it’s a page or a stage, rather than building rule systems, navigable spaces, and archives that fully exist only when someone interacts with them. This chapter leaves me energized and a little restless. Murray wrote, “yet the urge to stay additive is still everywhere”; The real challenge Murray poses is formal. Can we invent narrative structures that belong to computers the way the novel belonged to print or the blockbuster belonged to the moving image? That doesn’t feel like a theoretical question, it feels like the actual work ahead.

We Dwell in Possibility as queer gardening simulation

T"We Dwell in Possibility" is a queer gardening simulation game created over several months by Eleanor Davis, Sophia Foster-Dimino, and Andy Grier. The game consists of planting bodies and ideas, and watching them grow into a kinetic landscape.
