Constituents of a Theory of the Media

After sitting with Enzensberger’s “Constituents of a Theory of the Media” for some days, I’m not quite sure how to feel about it. On one side, I think it's uncanny how much he got right in 1970. He knew that the real issue wasn’t just “manipulation”, like in the sense the New Left kept talking about, its the media’s technical structure actually points toward something more participatory, meaning every “receiver” could theoretically become a “transmitter”. That feels prophetic when you look at our current situation with smart phones and social media today. At the same time, the way he frames the solution, which is organizing the masses to take over the means of production, feels incomplete. I wonder whether the problem is really only about ownership and organization, or if something about the form of the media themselves keeps producing passivity/apathy when people do start creating content.

What I think I appreciate most is how he pushes against the Left’s tendency to treat media as purely toxic and to retreat into older, “cleaner” forms of culture. I’ve also noticed the same impulse in my own life and in a lot of academic conversations; Here's this quiet longing for something more authentic/less contaminated than the digital mess we’re stuck in. Enzensberger says in a nutshell that attitude is a luxury we can’t afford and that it ends up handing the whole field over to capital. I’m not 100% convinced his alternative is fully workable. The challenge he poses feels important: if the means of production are already in hands of the masses, then why does the overall structure feel one-directional? That’s the question I keep coming back to.
